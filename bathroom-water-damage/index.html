Bathroom Water Damage in Taylor

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Taylor team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall

Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.
