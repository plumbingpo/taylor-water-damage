A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in Taylor, Taylor Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

After extraction and sanitizing, we set up commercial drying equipment, including LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, to dry the structure of your property thoroughly. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. From start to finish we document each step for your insurance claim, with the moisture maps, equipment logs, photos and scope-of-work reports adjusters ask for.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor

Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
