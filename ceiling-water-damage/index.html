Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in Taylor

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

Signs to Look For

  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels away from the ceiling surface
  • Drips coming out of a ceiling light, air vent or smoke alarm
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Older Ceiling Materials

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.
