Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Taylor

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Attic Insulation

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • How much flooring, wall and ceiling material needs replacement.
  • Whether the air handler is in the attic, a closet or a garage.
  • How dirty the condensate became while it sat, which sets the level of cleaning.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Spread

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
