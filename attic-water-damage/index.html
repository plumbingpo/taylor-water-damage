Attic Water Damage in Taylor

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Taylor inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.
