Damage That Is Often Covered

Sudden and accidental water damage is covered under the majority of standard homeowner's policies. Typical examples are a burst pipe, an ice dam, a sink or bathtub that overflows by accident, a sudden HVAC failure, and a failed dishwasher, washing machine, or water heater. Coverage typically extends to water extraction, structural drying, material replacement, and related restoration costs, including temporary living expenses if your home is uninhabitable.

Damage That Is Often Excluded

Several kinds of loss are normally left out: groundwater seepage at the foundation, long-term leaks, problems caused by putting off maintenance, floods from external sources (a separate flood policy is needed), and sewer backups (a specific endorsement is needed). Understanding these exclusions before a loss occurs lets you add endorsements or separate policies to fill coverage gaps.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

When water damage occurs, take these steps to protect your claim, starting with photos and video of everything before cleanup begins. Contact your insurance company promptly to report the loss. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Hold on to ruined belongings and materials so the adjuster can inspect them first. Have the work done by professionals who are IICRC-certified. Keep all receipts related to the loss, including temporary repairs and living expenses. When the adjuster visits, be there to walk them through all of the damage.
