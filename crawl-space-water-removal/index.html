Crawl Space Water Removal: What Taylor Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Warning Signs

  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Rot You Cannot See

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
