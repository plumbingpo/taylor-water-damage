Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Taylor

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
