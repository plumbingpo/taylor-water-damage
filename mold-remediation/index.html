Treat mold as a warning as well as a health hazard, because the hidden moisture feeding it puts your property's structural integrity at risk. Mold exposure can cause respiratory issues, allergic reactions, headaches and more severe health effects, particularly in children, the elderly and people with compromised immune systems. Taylor Restoration sends IICRC-certified technicians to homes and commercial buildings across Taylor, where they contain mold, remove it and keep it from returning under strict industry protocols.

Effective mold remediation goes far beyond scrubbing visible mold off surfaces. A professional job has five parts: locate the water source that feeds the growth, build proper containment against cross-contamination, use HEPA-filtered tools to remove damaged materials safely, treat surfaces left behind with an antimicrobial solution, and resolve the moisture problem at its root to stop recurrence. The result of that thorough process is mold dealt with at its source, never simply masked.

First comes a full assessment of the property, frequently with air quality tests that identify the kinds of spores in the air and how concentrated they are. With that data we can build a remediation plan aimed at exactly what your property needs. Throughout the project we maintain negative air pressure containment, run HEPA air scrubbers and follow the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. Afterward we verify the results, with clearance air testing if you request it, to confirm the property is safe to occupy.

When to Call for Mold Remediation in Taylor

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Signs of concealed moisture such as paint that bubbles, wallpaper that peels or materials that warp
  • Water damage not long ago that was left to dry without professional equipment
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Furniture against outside walls or items in closets developing speckled patches
  • Dark staining or discoloration on walls, ceilings or around HVAC vents
  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • Patches of mold, whatever the color, on a ceiling, wall, floor or other surface

How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Address condensation on windows, pipes and cold surfaces promptly
  • Mold can get going after just 24 to 48 hours of dampness, so have leaks fixed within 24 hours
  • Overflowing gutters send water into basements and crawl spaces, so clean them on a schedule
  • Keep indoor humidity below 60% (ideally 30 to 50%) with dehumidifiers and proper ventilation
  • Check that exhaust fans in the kitchen, laundry room and bathrooms move enough air
  • Dry wet rugs, towels and spills right away instead of letting them sit

How Our Mold Remediation Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle mold remediation from start to finish:

1

Inspection & Testing

A certified technician goes through the property to find mold both in plain view and out of sight, traces the moisture feeding it and, where useful, takes surface or air samples for a lab to analyze.

2

Containment & Filtration

HEPA air scrubbers and polyethylene barriers put the work zone under negative air pressure, which keeps spores from reaching clean parts of the building while we remove the mold.

3

Removing Mold and Treating Surfaces

Following EPA guidelines, our crew removes moldy material with care and handles its disposal. A professional-grade antimicrobial solution is then applied to the structural surfaces that stay.

4

Fixing the Moisture and Verifying Results

We address the underlying moisture source, complete any necessary drying and perform post-remediation verification to confirm the remediation succeeded before releasing containment.

What a Professional Brings to Mold Remediation

This is not a job to take on yourself. Disturbing mold colonies without proper containment sends millions of spores airborne, which can contaminate unaffected areas of your home and create worse health exposure than the original problem. Professional mold remediation uses negative air pressure containment, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers and personal protective equipment so spores are captured and contained during removal.

A professional job goes after the moisture source too, because that is why the mold grew in the first place. If the moisture is left alone, taking away what you can see only buys time, and regrowth often shows up within weeks. Finding concealed moisture, drying it properly and preventing a repeat all take equipment and training, which certified remediation technicians have.
